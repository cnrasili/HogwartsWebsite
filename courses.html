Courses

Seven years of magical study across core, elective, and advanced subjects

Core Subjects

All students study these seven subjects from Year 1, culminating in the Ordinary Wizarding Level (O.W.L.) examinations at the end of Year 5. Outstanding students may continue to N.E.W.T. level in Years 6 and 7.

Astronomy

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

The study of stars, planets, and their movements, conducted from the Astronomy Tower at midnight. Students learn to identify and chart celestial bodies and understand how the movements of heavenly objects influence the practice of magic.

Charms

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

A branch of magic concerned with adding properties to an object or creature without altering its fundamental nature. Students progress from Wingardium Leviosa and basic Summoning Charms through to complex nonverbal casting and the Fidelius Charm at N.E.W.T. level.

Defence Against the Dark Arts

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

Practical study of defensive spells and countercurses against Dark creatures and practitioners of the Dark Arts. Topics include Boggarts, Grindylows, Dementors, Unforgivable Curses, Occlumency, and advanced counter-Dark Arts strategy at N.E.W.T. level.

Herbology

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

The study and cultivation of magical plants and fungi, conducted in the school's greenhouses. Students handle specimens from common Mandrakes and Devil's Snare to rare potion-grade flora and the propagation of Venomous Tentacula at advanced level.

History of Magic

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.)

A study of the wizarding world throughout history. Topics include the Goblin Rebellions, the International Statute of Secrecy, the establishment of the Ministry of Magic, and key magical conflicts. The only class at Hogwarts taught by a ghost — Professor Binns.

Potions

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

The art of creating mixtures with magical effects through precise preparation and patient technique. No wand required. Covers simple Boil-Cure Potions and Sleeping Draughts in early years, progressing to Veritaserum, Amortentia, and Felix Felicis at N.E.W.T. level.

Transfiguration

Years 1 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

The art of changing the form or appearance of an object — one of the most complex and dangerous branches of magic. Students advance from simple match-to-needle work through Switching Spells and Vanishment to human Transfiguration and Animagus theory at N.E.W.T. level.

Elective Subjects

From Year 3, students choose at least two elective subjects to study alongside the core curriculum. Electives continue through Year 5 and culminate in O.W.L. examinations. At least two electives are strongly recommended for students pursuing specialist magical careers.

Arithmancy

Years 3 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

The study of the magical properties of numbers and their application to predicting future events. Considered the most intellectually demanding elective, Arithmancy requires strong logical thinking and is essential preparation for careers in Curse Breaking and advanced spell theory.

Care of Magical Creatures

Years 3 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

A hands-on outdoor course covering the habitats, behaviour, and handling of magical beasts. Students work directly with creatures including Hippogriffs, Blast-Ended Skrewts, Nifflers, and Flobberworms. Practical competence with live creatures is assessed throughout.

Divination

Years 3 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

The art of predicting the future through tasseomancy (tea leaf reading), crystal ball gazing, fire omens, astrology, and dream interpretation. Taught in the North Tower by Professor Trelawney. The subject is considered intuitive in nature and results vary considerably between students.

Muggle Studies

Years 3 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

An examination of the non-magical world from a wizarding perspective. Students study how Muggles live without magic — their technology, culture, and societal structures. The subject is particularly valued by students interested in Muggle liaison work at the Ministry of Magic.

Study of Ancient Runes

Years 3 – 5 (O.W.L.); Years 6 – 7 (N.E.W.T.)

A study of runic scripts, their translation, and their application as magical symbols. Students work extensively with ancient texts and learn how runes are used to inscribe and activate enchantments. Essential for students intending to pursue Curse Breaking or advanced spellcraft research.

Advanced Electives — Years 6 & 7

A small number of specialist elective subjects are available exclusively to sixth and seventh-year students. These subjects require either a strong O.W.L. record or special authorisation from the relevant professor and the Headmaster.

Advanced Arithmancy Studies

Years 6 – 7

A continuation beyond standard Arithmancy O.W.L., exploring the use of complex number theory to model and predict large-scale magical phenomena. Students undertake independent research projects and study historical applications of numerical enchantment in magical architecture and ward construction.

Alchemy

Years 6 – 7

The ancient study of the transformation of matter and the pursuit of the Philosopher's Stone. Students examine the theoretical and historical foundations of alchemical practice, the relationship between Alchemy and Transfiguration, and the work of notable alchemists including Nicolas Flamel.

Apparition

Year 6 (one term)

A Ministry-licensed course teaching the magical skill of instantaneous transportation from one location to another. Conducted over one term in the Great Hall under the supervision of a Ministry Apparition instructor. Students must be 17 or older to take their Apparition licence test.

Other Classes

Classes that fall outside the standard core and elective curriculum, offered under specific circumstances or to particular year groups.

Dark Arts

Year 6 only (1997–1998 academic year)

Taught briefly during the Death Eater-controlled administration of 1997–1998 as a replacement for Defence Against the Dark Arts. The course focused on practising Dark curses rather than defending against them and was abolished upon the restoration of the school following the Battle of Hogwarts.

Flying

Year 1 only

An introductory course in broomstick flight taught on the school grounds by Madam Hooch. Students learn to take off, hover, manoeuvre, and land safely. Exceptional ability in Flying may result in selection for a house Quidditch team. Not assessed by O.W.L. or N.E.W.T. examinations.

Extra-Curricular Subjects

Hogwarts offers a range of optional extra-curricular subjects for students who wish to broaden their education beyond the standard curriculum. These subjects carry no O.W.L. or N.E.W.T. assessment and are available to various year groups subject to scheduling.

Ancient Studies

Available to all year groups

An exploration of ancient magical civilisations and their practices, including the magical traditions of Egypt, Greece, and early wizarding Britain. Students examine primary sources and magical artefacts to understand how ancient wizarding cultures shaped modern magical knowledge.

Art

Available to all year groups

A creative subject covering both traditional and magical art forms. Students work with enchanted canvases, self-inking quills, and colour-changing pigments. Subjects include magical portraiture, the tradition of moving paintings, and the art of illustrating spell texts.

Field Studies

Available to all year groups

Practical guided expeditions into the surrounding environment, including the Forbidden Forest and the grounds of Hogwarts. Students observe magical plants, creatures, and natural phenomena in their native settings under close supervision of experienced staff members.

Ghoul Studies

Available to all year groups

An examination of ghouls, poltergeists, and other minor non-beings found in wizarding households and older buildings. Students learn to distinguish between different categories of entities, understand their behaviours, and study appropriate management and cohabitation strategies.

Magical Theory

Available to all year groups

A theoretical introduction to the principles underlying magical practice — particularly useful for Muggle-born students beginning their magical education. Covers the nature of wand magic, the relationship between intent and spell outcome, and the historical development of wand-based casting.

Muggle Art

Available to all year groups

A survey of Muggle visual art history and practice, from cave paintings to the modern era. Students examine how Muggle artists create without magic and explore the cultural significance of non-magical artworks. Often taken alongside Muggle Studies as a complementary elective.

Muggle Music

Available to all year groups

An introduction to non-magical musical traditions from various cultures and periods. Students listen to and analyse Muggle compositions, study musical instruments that function without enchantment, and explore how Muggle music has at times intersected with or influenced wizarding musical traditions.

Music

Available to all year groups

Practical and theoretical study of wizarding musical traditions. Students learn to play enchanted instruments including self-tuning lutes, whispering harps, and the Fwooper-feather flute. Covers harmony theory, the magical properties of certain musical scales, and ensemble performance.

Xylomancy

Available to all year groups

The art of divination through the interpretation of wood — its grain, knots, bark, and the patterns formed by fallen branches. One of the oldest forms of wizarding divination, Xylomancy is closely related to the study of wand wood properties and has applications in wandcraft and forest magic.
