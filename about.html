About & History

Over a thousand years of magical education in the heart of Scotland

Our History

Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry was founded around 990 A.D. by four of the greatest witches and wizards of the age: Godric Gryffindor, Salazar Slytherin, Rowena Ravenclaw, and Helga Hufflepuff. Together they chose a remote location in the Scottish Highlands, far from Muggle settlements, to establish a school where young witches and wizards could receive a safe and rigorous magical education.

For over a millennium, Hogwarts has stood as Britain's foremost institution of magical learning. Its enchanted castle, ancient corridors, and moving staircases have sheltered generations of students, guiding them through seven transformative years of study and growth. The school's motto, Draco Dormiens Nunquam Titillandus — "Never Tickle a Sleeping Dragon" — reflects both its wisdom and its wit.

The early centuries of the school were marked by expansion and consolidation. The library, the greenhouses, and the Astronomy Tower were all established within the first two hundred years. The four founders' original vision — a place of rigorous learning and genuine community — shaped every stone of the castle and every tradition that endures to this day.

Throughout the centuries, Hogwarts has weathered threats both internal and external. The construction and concealment of the Chamber of Secrets, the imposition of the International Statute of Secrecy, the rise and fall of Lord Voldemort, and the Battle of Hogwarts itself have each left their mark on the school's fabric and its memory. Each time, the school has endured — restored by the dedication of its staff and the courage of its students.

Historical Timeline

Key events in the thousand-year history of Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry.

c. 990 A.D.
School Founded

Godric Gryffindor, Salazar Slytherin, Rowena Ravenclaw, and Helga Hufflepuff establish Hogwarts in the Scottish Highlands. The four founders each select students according to the qualities they most admire.

c. 993 A.D.
Slytherin's Departure

Following a bitter dispute over the admission of Muggle-born students, Salazar Slytherin secretly constructs the Chamber of Secrets beneath the school and departs Hogwarts permanently. The chamber remains hidden for nearly a thousand years.

1692
International Statute of Secrecy

The International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy is enacted. Hogwarts strengthens its concealment enchantments, rendering the castle invisible to Muggle eyes and unplottable on Muggle maps. The Hogwarts Express is established in the following century to transport students discreetly.

c. 1790
Hogwarts Express Established

The Ministry of Magic arranges a dedicated steam locomotive service to transport students from London to Hogsmeade Station each September. Platform Nine and Three-Quarters at King's Cross Station is enchanted as the boarding point.

1943
Chamber of Secrets — First Opening

The Chamber of Secrets is opened for the first time in a thousand years by sixteen-year-old Tom Marvolo Riddle. Several students are Petrified and one student, Moaning Myrtle, is killed. Rubeus Hagrid is wrongly blamed and expelled.

1956
Albus Dumbledore Appointed Headmaster

Professor Albus Dumbledore, widely regarded as the greatest wizard of the age, becomes Headmaster of Hogwarts. His long tenure transforms the school's culture and ensures it remains a place of inclusive magical education.

1992–93
Chamber of Secrets Reopened

The Chamber of Secrets is opened a second time using Tom Riddle's enchanted diary. Multiple students and the school's caretaker's cat are Petrified. The Basilisk is ultimately defeated in the chamber beneath the school, and the diary is destroyed.

1994–95
The Triwizard Tournament

Hogwarts hosts the Triwizard Tournament for the first time in over a century, welcoming students from Beauxbatons Academy and Durmstrang Institute. The tournament ends in tragedy when Lord Voldemort returns to full power in the graveyard of Little Hangleton.

1995–96
Ministry Interference

The Ministry of Magic, refusing to acknowledge Voldemort's return, appoints Dolores Umbridge as Defence Against the Dark Arts teacher and later as High Inquisitor. Dumbledore flees the school briefly. Umbridge assumes the role of Headmistress before ultimately being removed.

May 1998
The Battle of Hogwarts

Voldemort's forces storm the castle in the decisive final battle of the Second Wizarding War. Students, staff, and Order of the Phoenix members defend the school. Voldemort is defeated and killed. The castle sustains significant damage, which is subsequently repaired through magical restoration.

The Four Founders

Gryffindor

Godric Gryffindor

The most celebrated of the four founders, Gryffindor prized courage, bravery, and nerve above all else. He championed the admission of Muggle-born students and his personal sword — a goblin-made blade set with rubies — became a treasured Hogwarts relic.

Courage Bravery Chivalry
Slytherin

Salazar Slytherin

A Parselmouth and one of the most brilliant wizards of his generation, Slytherin sought students of pure magical heritage. He constructed the Chamber of Secrets beneath the school before departing following a schism with the other founders.

Cunning Ambition Resourcefulness
Ravenclaw

Rowena Ravenclaw

Widely regarded as the cleverest of the founders, Ravenclaw valued intelligence, creativity, and wisdom. According to legend, the ingenious layout of Hogwarts castle itself was conceived in a dream she had after following a warty hog to a lake.

Wit Wisdom Creativity
Hufflepuff

Helga Hufflepuff

The most inclusive of the four founders, Hufflepuff welcomed students of all backgrounds, valuing hard work, loyalty, and fair play. She also contributed enormously to the school's early community and many of the Hogwarts feast recipes are attributed to her.

Loyalty Patience Hard Work

Notable Headmasters & Headmistresses

Hogwarts has been led by many distinguished witches and wizards over its thousand-year history. Their portraits line the Headmaster's office, offering counsel to those who serve after them.

c. 1200s
Eupraxia Mole

Negotiated the first formal agreement with the Hogsmeade village residents, establishing the boundaries of school authority and granting students limited access to the village in their later years.

Late 1800s – Early 1900s
Phineas Nigellus Black

Widely regarded as the least popular Headmaster in Hogwarts history. A member of the Black family and a proud Slytherin, he governed with strict discipline and little warmth. His portrait remains in the Headmaster's office.

1930s – 1950s
Armando Dippet

Headmaster during the years Tom Riddle attended Hogwarts. A mild-mannered and aging wizard, Dippet was deceived by Riddle during the first opening of the Chamber of Secrets and narrowly avoided closing the school.

1956 – 1997
Albus Percival Wulfric Brian Dumbledore

Considered the greatest Headmaster in Hogwarts history and by many the greatest wizard of the age. His tenure transformed the school into a symbol of inclusive magical education and resistance against the Dark Arts. Defeater of the dark wizard Grindelwald in 1945.

1997 – 1998
Severus Snape

Appointed Headmaster under Voldemort's regime, Snape covertly protected students from Death Eater staff throughout his tenure. His true loyalty — to Dumbledore and to the cause against Voldemort — was revealed only after his death at the Battle of Hogwarts.

1998 – Present
Minerva McGonagall

Appointed Headmistress following the Battle of Hogwarts and the restoration of the school. A distinguished Transfiguration scholar, former Head of Gryffindor House, and Deputy Headmistress for over three decades, she leads Hogwarts into its next era.

The Castle

Hogwarts castle is a vast, many-turreted structure perched on a cliff above the Black Lake, accessible only by magical means or the Hogwarts Express from King's Cross Station, Platform 9¾. The castle is enchanted to appear as a dangerous ruin to Muggle eyes and cannot be located on any Muggle map.

The Great Hall

The central gathering place for all students and staff. Its enchanted ceiling reflects the weather above and four long house tables line its floor. Home to the Sorting Ceremony, all communal feasts, and O.W.L. examinations.

The Library

Home to tens of thousands of volumes covering every branch of magic. The Restricted Section requires a signed permission slip from a teacher. Madam Pince has overseen the collection for several decades.

Astronomy Tower

The tallest tower of the castle, used exclusively for Astronomy lessons conducted at midnight. Students observe and chart celestial bodies from its open platform using telescopes and enchanted star maps.

Quidditch Pitch

A large oval stadium on the castle grounds where inter-house Quidditch matches are played throughout the academic year. Seven tall hooped goal posts stand at each end, surrounded by tiered spectator stands.

The Forbidden Forest

A vast, ancient forest bordering the school grounds, home to centaurs, thestrals, unicorns, Acromantulas, and many other magical creatures. Entry is strictly forbidden to students without staff supervision.

Room of Requirement

A hidden chamber on the seventh floor that only appears when a person is in true need of it. The room transforms itself to suit any purpose — a practice space, a hiding place, or a store for objects that need to be concealed.

Hospital Wing

Located on the first floor, the Hospital Wing is staffed by Madam Pomfrey and treats students for injuries and ailments ranging from Quidditch accidents and hexes to the after-effects of poorly brewed potions.

The Black Lake

A deep, cold lake bordering the castle grounds, home to a Giant Squid, Merpeople, Grindylows, and other aquatic creatures. First-year students cross it by boat on the night of their arrival at Hogwarts.

Hogwarts School

Providing magical education since 990 A.D. Located in the Scottish Highlands.
